Pick up a camera and it moves. Your pulse moves it, your breathing moves it, and the act of
pressing the button moves it. If the shutter is open long enough for that movement to matter, the
whole frame smears.

This is <GlossaryLink term="camera-shake" />, and it is probably the single most common reason a
beginner's photographs are not sharp.

## Telling it apart from the alternatives

Three different faults look like "blurry" and have three different cures, so it is worth learning
to tell them apart.

| What you see | What it is | What fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything soft, including still things | Camera shake | Faster shutter, or support |
| Subject smeared, background sharp | <GlossaryLink term="motion-blur" /> | Faster shutter |
| Something else sharp instead | Missed <GlossaryLink term="focus" /> | Focus on the right thing |

The tell for camera shake is that things which were definitely not moving — a wall, a table — are
soft as well.

## How fast is fast enough

The rule of thumb is the <GlossaryLink term="reciprocal-rule" />: use a shutter speed at least as
fast as one over the <GlossaryLink term="focal-length" />.

- 24mm lens → 1/25s or faster
- 50mm lens → 1/50s or faster
- 200mm lens → 1/200s or faster

A longer lens magnifies your movement exactly as much as it magnifies the subject, which is why it
demands more.

<Callout type="note">
  It is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee. It assumes decent technique and no stabilisation, and it
  was devised before sensors detailed enough to show smaller movements. Treat it as the slowest
  speed worth attempting.
</Callout>

## Technique that costs nothing

- Bring your elbows in against your body rather than winging them out.
- Support the lens from underneath with your left hand, rather than gripping it from the side.
- Breathe out and take the shot at the bottom of the breath.
- Squeeze the button rather than stabbing it.
- Lean on something. A doorframe, a wall, a car roof — anything that is not you.

None of these require buying anything, and together they are worth roughly a stop.
