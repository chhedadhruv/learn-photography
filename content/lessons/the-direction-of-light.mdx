Move around a subject and the light changes character entirely, without the light itself changing at
all. Three broad positions, three different photographs.

## Front light

The light behind you, falling on the subject's face.

Even, uncomplicated, easy to expose. It also flattens: with shadows falling directly away from the
camera, there is nothing left to describe shape or texture.

Useful when you want information rather than atmosphere. It is why identification photographs are
lit this way.

## Side light

The light coming across the subject.

Shadows fall across the surface, which reveals texture and shape. Stone looks like stone; a face
gains structure.

It is also the most demanding to expose, because the lit and shadowed sides can differ by several
<GlossaryLink term="stop" />s. Either accept the contrast, or add <GlossaryLink term="fill-light" />
to lift the dark side.

<Callout type="tip">
  Full side light is dramatic and unflattering in equal measure. Rotating the subject slightly
  towards the light keeps most of the shape and loses most of the harshness.
</Callout>

## Back light

The light behind the subject, facing you.

<GlossaryLink term="backlighting" /> rims edges with brightness, separates the subject from its
background, and makes translucent things — leaves, hair, glasses of water — glow.

It also guarantees a fight with the meter. The camera reads a very bright frame and underexposes the
subject. Use spot <GlossaryLink term="metering" /> or positive
<GlossaryLink term="exposure-compensation" /> unless a silhouette is what you wanted.

Watch for flare. Light striking the front element directly washes out contrast; a lens hood, or a
hand held just outside the frame, usually fixes it.

## Choosing

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity and information | Front |
| Texture and shape | Side |
| Separation and atmosphere | Back |

You usually cannot move the sun. You can almost always move yourself, or the subject.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Front light is even and flat; side light describes shape; back light separates and glows.
- Side light is the most demanding to expose and often needs fill.
- Backlighting reliably fools a meter — compensate or spot-meter.
- Changing direction usually means moving yourself, not the light.

</KeyTakeaways>
