Most of what makes a portrait work is not technical. But the technical part is small and worth
getting out of the way.

## Settings

**Aperture** wide enough to separate the subject from the background — f/2 to f/4 is typical — but
not so wide that only one eye is sharp. At f/1.4 the <GlossaryLink term="depth-of-field" /> can be
shallower than the distance between someone's eyes.

**Shutter speed** at least 1/125 for someone standing still. People move more than they think, and
so do you.

**Focal length** between 50mm and 135mm on <GlossaryLink term="full-frame" />. Wider than that
enlarges whatever is nearest the camera, which at portrait distance is the nose. Longer means
standing far enough away that conversation becomes awkward.

## Focus on the near eye

At these apertures, focus precisely. Viewers look at eyes first and forgive softness nearly
everywhere else — but a portrait with the far eye sharp and the near one soft reads as a mistake to
people who could not explain why.

Use a single focus point and put it on the eye. Not the face, not the head. The eye.

## Light

Soft light from slightly above and to one side flatters nearly everyone. That is a window, or open
shade, or an overcast sky.

Avoid overhead midday sun, which puts shadows into eye sockets and drops the nose's shadow onto the
mouth. If you cannot avoid it, move into shade — see
[daylight through the day](/learn/daylight-through-the-day).

<Callout type="tip">
  Turn the subject slightly towards the light rather than facing it square on. Enough angle to give
  the face shape, not enough to leave half of it dark.
</Callout>

## The part that is not technical

A photograph of someone who is uncomfortable looks like a photograph of someone who is uncomfortable,
whatever the settings.

- Decide your settings before they are standing there waiting.
- Talk. Long silences make people self-conscious.
- Give something to do rather than a pose to hold — walking, sitting down, looking away and back.
- Take more frames than feel necessary. Expressions that work last fractions of a second.

<KeyTakeaways>

- f/2 to f/4 separates without making focus unreliable.
- Focus on the nearest eye, with a single point.
- Soft light from slightly above and to one side suits almost everyone.
- Comfort matters more than settings; sort the settings out beforehand.

</KeyTakeaways>
