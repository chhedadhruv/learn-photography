The shutter is a curtain in front of the sensor. Shutter speed is how long it stays open, and it is
the control whose effect is easiest to see.

## The numbers

Written as fractions of a second: 1/1000, 1/500, 1/250, and so on down to whole seconds. Each step
in that sequence halves or doubles the time, and so halves or doubles the light — one
<GlossaryLink term="stop" />.

The printed numbers are rounded. A camera marked 1/125 is really giving you 1/128; the sequence is
powers of two underneath, which is what makes the stops exact.

## Freezing and smearing

Anything that moves while the shutter is open records in more than one place, and that reads as a
smear along the direction of travel.

| Subject | Roughly enough to freeze |
| --- | --- |
| A person standing, small movements | 1/60 |
| Someone walking | 1/250 |
| Someone running | 1/500 |
| A cyclist or a car at speed | 1/1000 or faster |

Three things push those numbers up: a faster subject, a closer subject, and a longer lens. All
three amount to the same thing — how quickly the subject crosses the frame.

<Callout type="note">
  Movement across your view smears far more than movement towards you. A car driving past needs a
  much faster shutter than the same car driving at you.
</Callout>

## Blur on purpose

A smear is not a fault unless you did not want it. Slow shutter speeds are how flowing water
becomes silk, how traffic becomes ribbons of light, and how a photograph says that something was
moving.

The condition is that everything else stays sharp. That means the camera cannot move — so a
tripod, or a wall, or anything solid. Otherwise you get <GlossaryLink term="camera-shake" /> on top
of the intended <GlossaryLink term="motion-blur" />, and the result just looks like a mistake.

## The cost

Every stop of shutter speed you gain has to come from somewhere. Doubling the speed halves the
light, so it must be paid for with a wider <GlossaryLink term="aperture" /> or a higher
<GlossaryLink term="iso" />.

This is the trade you will make most often. Freezing a subject in poor light is exactly the
situation where all three controls fight each other.
