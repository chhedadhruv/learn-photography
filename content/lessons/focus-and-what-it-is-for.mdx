A lens forms a sharp image at exactly one distance. Focusing is choosing that distance.

Everything nearer or further is technically not in focus. <GlossaryLink term="depth-of-field" />
is the band either side where it is close enough that nobody notices — but there is still only one
plane that is genuinely sharp.

## The part the camera cannot do

Modern autofocus is fast and accurate. What it cannot do is know which part of the scene you care
about.

Left to itself, a camera tends to pick whatever is nearest, or highest in contrast, or in the
middle of the frame. For a portrait against a busy street that is often a lamppost.

The fix is to choose the focus point yourself rather than letting the camera choose. On most
cameras this means switching from an automatic area mode to single-point, and then moving that
point onto the thing that matters.

<Callout type="tip">
  For a portrait, the thing that matters is the eye nearest the camera. If the eyes are sharp, a
  photograph forgives a great deal elsewhere; if they are not, nothing else rescues it.
</Callout>

## Single and continuous

Two modes, and the choice is about whether the distance is changing.

- **Single** focuses once and locks. Right for anything still: a landscape, a building, a person
  sitting down.
- **Continuous** keeps adjusting for as long as you half-press. Right for anything moving towards
  or away from you, because by the time a single lock has happened the distance has changed.

Sideways movement matters much less than movement towards you. Someone walking across your view
stays roughly the same distance away; someone walking towards you does not.

## Focus and sharpness are different things

A photograph can be perfectly focused and still not sharp — blurred by
<GlossaryLink term="camera-shake" />, or by the subject moving. Conversely it can be pin-sharp and
focused on the wrong thing.

Worth diagnosing separately, because the cures are unrelated.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Only one distance is truly sharp; depth of field is the band that looks sharp enough.
- Autofocus is accurate but does not know what you meant — choose the point yourself.
- Single autofocus for still subjects, continuous for anything moving towards you.
- For portraits, focus on the nearest eye.

</KeyTakeaways>
