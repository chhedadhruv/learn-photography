One light source, entirely different results depending on the hour. The pattern is predictable, which
means it can be planned for.

## Dawn and dusk: the golden hour

When the sun is low, its light passes through much more atmosphere. That scatters out the blue,
leaving warm <GlossaryLink term="colour-temperature" />, and softens the light considerably.

It also arrives from the side, which reveals texture and shape rather than flattening them.

<GlossaryLink term="golden-hour" /> is the most flattering light most photographers ever get for
free. Its drawback is that it changes quickly and does not wait.

## Before dawn and after dusk: the blue hour

Once the sun is below the horizon, the light comes from the whole sky rather than from the sun. It
is even, shadowless and strongly blue.

<GlossaryLink term="blue-hour" /> is the best time to photograph a city, because artificial lights
are lit and still balance against the sky. Half an hour later they overwhelm it.

There is very little of it, and it needs a tripod or a high <GlossaryLink term="iso" />.

## Midday

The sun overhead, small in the sky and unfiltered. Hard light, high contrast, shadows pooling
directly beneath things.

It is genuinely difficult for faces — eye sockets go dark, noses cast shadows onto mouths. It is
good for graphic shadows, for architecture, and for anything where contrast is the subject.

<Callout type="tip">
  The midday answer is usually shade. Open shade — lit by the sky but not the sun — is soft, even
  and available in every doorway.
</Callout>

## Overcast

Cloud turns the entire sky into one enormous soft source. Shadows almost disappear, contrast drops,
and colours saturate.

Unhelpful for drama; excellent for portraits, for detail, and for anything in a forest, where direct
sun would produce a mess of blown highlights and black shadows.

## Planning

| Time | Light | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue hour | Even, blue, dim | Cities, water, skylines |
| Golden hour | Warm, soft, low | Portraits, landscape, texture |
| Midday | Hard, overhead | Graphic shadows, shade, interiors |
| Overcast | Soft, even | Portraits, detail, woodland |

The useful habit is checking sunrise and sunset times for where you are, and arriving before rather
than during.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Low sun is warm and soft because its light travels through more atmosphere.
- Blue hour is short, even and ideal for cities.
- Midday is hard and unflattering for faces; open shade is the answer.
- Overcast is a giant soft source, poor for drama and excellent for detail.

</KeyTakeaways>
