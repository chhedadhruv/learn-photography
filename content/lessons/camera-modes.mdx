The mode dial is not a difficulty ladder with automatic at one end and expertise at the other. Each
mode hands you one control and works out the others.

## What each one does

| Mode | You set | Camera sets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P** — Program | Nothing; you can shift the pairing | Aperture and shutter |
| **A** or **Av** — Aperture priority | <GlossaryLink term="aperture" /> | Shutter |
| **S** or **Tv** — Shutter priority | <GlossaryLink term="shutter-speed" /> | Aperture |
| **M** — Manual | Both | Nothing |

<GlossaryLink term="iso" /> sits outside this and can be automatic or fixed in any of them.

Canon writes Av and Tv where most others write A and S. Same modes, different labels.

## Choosing between them

The useful question is: which control does this photograph depend on?

**Aperture priority** when <GlossaryLink term="depth-of-field" /> is what matters — portraits,
landscapes, anything where the background is part of the decision. This is what most working
photographers leave their cameras in.

**Shutter priority** when movement is what matters — sport, children, running water. You are
saying "whatever else happens, do not go slower than this".

**Manual** when the light is constant and you want it to stop changing between frames, or when the
scene keeps fooling the meter and you are tired of arguing with it via
<GlossaryLink term="exposure-compensation" />.

**Program** when you want the camera to get you something usable while you think about where to
stand.

<Callout type="tip">
  Aperture priority plus exposure compensation covers most photography. It is not a beginner's
  compromise — it is faster than manual and the camera is right most of the time.
</Callout>

## Auto ISO changes the picture

Turning ISO to automatic changes what these modes mean. In aperture priority with auto ISO, the
camera has two controls to play with, so it will hold a sensible shutter speed and raise the ISO
rather than letting the shutter drag.

Most cameras let you set a minimum shutter speed for this, which is worth finding. It turns
aperture priority into something close to "choose the depth of field and never let me get camera
shake", which is a genuinely useful thing to have.

## The scene modes

The pictograms — running figure, flower, mountain — are aperture or shutter priority with the
decision made for you. The running figure is shutter priority. The flower is a wide aperture.

Nothing wrong with them, but they hide which control they are moving, which makes them poor for
learning. You already know what they know.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Each mode gives you one control and calculates the rest.
- Aperture priority when depth of field matters; shutter priority when movement does.
- Manual is for constant light or a meter that keeps guessing wrong, not for showing off.
- Auto ISO turns aperture priority into a much more capable mode.

</KeyTakeaways>
