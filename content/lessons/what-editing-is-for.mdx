Every digital photograph is processed. The only question is whether the camera does it or you do.

A <GlossaryLink term="jpeg" /> straight out of a camera has already had white balance, contrast,
saturation and sharpening applied according to somebody's idea of what looks good. Editing a
<GlossaryLink term="raw" /> file is not adding interpretation — it is choosing it.

## What it does well

- **Exposure**, within about a stop either way.
- **<GlossaryLink term="white-balance" />**, completely and without penalty on a RAW file.
- **<GlossaryLink term="contrast" />** and the relationship between tones.
- **Cropping and straightening.**
- **Local adjustments** — brightening a face, darkening a distracting corner.

## What it cannot do

- **Recover <GlossaryLink term="clipping" />.** Pure white has no detail to bring back. Pulling the
  highlight slider down turns it grey, which is worse.
- **Undo blur.** Nothing recovers <GlossaryLink term="camera-shake" /> or missed
  <GlossaryLink term="focus" />. Sharpening increases edge contrast; it does not add information.
- **Fix composition.** Cropping changes framing but not the relationships between things, which
  needed you to move at the time.
- **Make a boring photograph interesting.**

<Callout type="note">
  The most useful thing editing teaches is what to do differently next time. If you find yourself
  making the same correction repeatedly, the fix belongs in the camera.
</Callout>

## How much is too much

There is no correct amount, but there is a useful test: does the edit serve the photograph, or is it
covering for it?

Heavy editing on a strong photograph is a style. Heavy editing on a weak one is usually an attempt to
make it into something else, and it rarely succeeds.

## Keep the original

Edit non-destructively — every serious tool does this by default, storing your changes as
instructions rather than altering the file.

Keep the RAW files. Your taste will change, tools improve, and a photograph you gave up on in year
one is sometimes recoverable in year three.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Every digital photograph is interpreted; editing means choosing the interpretation.
- It handles exposure, white balance, contrast and crop well.
- It cannot recover clipping, undo blur, or fix composition.
- Repeated corrections are a sign of something to change in the camera.

</KeyTakeaways>
