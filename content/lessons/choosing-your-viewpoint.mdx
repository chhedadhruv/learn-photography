Two ways to make a subject larger in the frame: zoom in, or walk closer. They are not the same
thing, and understanding the difference is worth more than any composition rule.

## What zooming does

Zooming crops. A longer <GlossaryLink term="focal-length" /> keeps a narrower slice of the same
view from the same place, so everything in it — subject and background alike — is enlarged
together.

The relationships stay exactly as they were.

## What moving does

Move closer and the subject grows faster than the background does, because you have changed its
distance far more than theirs proportionally. The background appears to shrink and spread out.

Move back and zoom in to compensate, and the reverse happens: the background looms, appearing
closer and larger behind the subject. This is why long lenses are said to compress distance.

<Callout type="tip">
  This is the only tool that changes what is behind your subject. A different aperture blurs a
  background; only moving replaces it.
</Callout>

## Height

Most photographs are taken from adult standing height, because that is where the photographer's
eyes were.

- **Lower** makes a subject look larger and more imposing, and puts more sky behind it.
- **Higher** shows layout and pattern, and separates things that overlap at eye level.
- **Level with the subject** is almost always right for children and animals, and almost never what
  people actually do.

## A habit worth building

Before taking the obvious frame from where you are standing, take three steps in some direction and
look again. Then crouch.

It costs seconds. It is also the single most reliable way to come back with a photograph that does
not look like everyone else's photograph of the same thing.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Zooming crops; moving changes the relationships between near and far.
- Only changing position can change what is behind your subject.
- Long lenses appear to compress distance because of where you stand to use them.
- Camera height is a decision, and standing height is rarely the most interesting one.

</KeyTakeaways>
