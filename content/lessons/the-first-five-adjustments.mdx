Editing goes wrong when it becomes a hunt through sliders. A short routine, in a fixed order, covers
most of what a photograph needs and finishes.

## 1. Crop and straighten

First, because everything after this depends on what is in the frame.

Straighten horizons — a tilted sea is distracting in a way a tilted building is not. Then remove
anything at the edges that survived the shot, and reconsider where the subject sits.

## 2. White balance

Second, because colour affects how you judge everything else.

Set <GlossaryLink term="white-balance" /> so that things which were neutral look neutral. Most tools
have an eyedropper: click something that should be grey or white.

Then, if you want to, deviate deliberately. Warming a sunset past neutral is a choice; being unable
to tell that it is warm is not.

## 3. Exposure and contrast

Set overall brightness so the subject reads correctly — not the whole frame, the subject.

Then <GlossaryLink term="contrast" />. Watch the <GlossaryLink term="histogram" /> as you go, and
watch the ends: pushing detail into pure black or pure white is throwing it away.

## 4. Highlights and shadows

Where the finer tonal work happens. Pulling highlights down recovers detail in bright areas; lifting
shadows opens up dark ones.

Both have limits. Highlights that are truly <GlossaryLink term="clipping" />ed do not come back, and
lifting shadows too far reveals <GlossaryLink term="noise" />.

<Callout type="tip">
  Take a break between adjusting and judging. Eyes adapt to whatever is on screen within seconds, so
  the edit that looked right after two minutes often looks overcooked the next day.
</Callout>

## 5. Local adjustments

Last, and only if the photograph needs them. Brightening a face, darkening a bright corner, adding
clarity to one area.

Local work is where editing gets slow, so it is worth being deliberate: fix a specific problem you
can name, then stop.

## Sharpening, briefly

Sharpening is not part of the routine because it depends on where the photograph is going. Sharpen
last, for the size it will be viewed at, and less than you think.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Crop, white balance, exposure and contrast, highlights and shadows, then local work.
- The order matters because each step changes how you judge the next.
- Expose for the subject, not the whole frame.
- Take a break before deciding the edit is finished.

</KeyTakeaways>
