Cameras are described in terms of settings, but settings are a response to light. Learning to look
at the light first is what makes the settings obvious rather than arbitrary.

Three things are worth judging before anything else.

## Hardness

The <GlossaryLink term="quality-of-light" /> is how abruptly it falls off into shadow.

**Hard light** comes from a small source: the midday sun, a bare bulb, a direct flash. Shadow edges
are crisp and contrast is high.

**Soft light** comes from a large source: an overcast sky, a north-facing window, light bounced off
a wall. Shadows fade gradually.

What decides it is the *apparent* size of the source. The sun is vast and gives hard light, because
it is so distant that it is small in the sky. Put a cloud in front of it and the whole cloud becomes
the source — which is why overcast light is soft.

<Callout type="tip">
  You can judge this without any equipment. Hold your hand out and look at the shadow's edge. Sharp
  edge, hard light. Gradual edge, soft light.
</Callout>

## Direction

Where the light is coming from decides what is revealed and what is hidden. That is enough of a
subject on its own — see [the direction of light](/learn/the-direction-of-light).

## Range

<GlossaryLink term="dynamic-range" /> is the span between the darkest and brightest parts of the
scene, and whether your sensor can hold all of it.

Stand indoors looking out of a window and your eyes read both the room and the view. A camera must
choose. When a scene holds more range than the sensor can record, something
<GlossaryLink term="clipping" />s.

The responses, in order of usefulness:

1. Change the light — fill the shadows, wait for cloud, come back later.
2. Change the framing to exclude the extreme.
3. Decide which end matters and let the other go.

## The question that shortcuts all of this

"Where is the light coming from, and how hard is it?"

Ask it before you raise the camera. Most exposure decisions answer themselves once you have.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Judge hardness by shadow edges; it depends on the apparent size of the source.
- The sun is hard because it is distant; cloud makes it soft by becoming the source.
- Scenes often hold more range than a sensor can record, and clipping is permanent.
- Ask where the light comes from and how hard it is before touching a dial.

</KeyTakeaways>
