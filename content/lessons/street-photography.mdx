Street photography is mostly about being ready. The moment does not wait while you adjust anything.

## Settings that stay put

The aim is not needing to change anything.

- **Aperture priority** at around f/8. Enough <GlossaryLink term="depth-of-field" /> that
  approximate focus is good enough.
- **Auto <GlossaryLink term="iso" />** with a minimum shutter speed of 1/250. Fast enough to freeze
  walking, and the camera raises sensitivity rather than letting the shutter drag.
- **Continuous focus**, single point, roughly central.

That combination handles most of what a street throws at you without touching a dial. See
[the letters on the mode dial](/learn/camera-modes).

<Callout type="tip">
  Set this up before you leave. Fiddling with a camera on a street corner is both slow and
  conspicuous.
</Callout>

## Working

**Find a place and wait.** Interesting light, a good background, a doorway people pass through.
Waiting for a subject to enter a composition you have already worked out is far more productive than
hunting for complete scenes.

**Get closer than feels comfortable.** Most street photographs fail from too much distance, not too
little.

**Keep the camera up.** Raising it is the moment people notice; a camera already at chest height
attracts far less attention.

## Light

Cities give hard light and deep shadow, which is often the point. Pools of light between buildings,
reflections off glass, a lit shopfront at dusk.

<GlossaryLink term="blue-hour" /> is particularly good in a city, when the artificial lights are lit
and still balance against the sky.

## Other people

Laws differ by country — check yours rather than assuming.

Beyond the law: photographing strangers involves a person who did not ask to be involved. A few
things that cost nothing —

- If someone notices and looks unhappy, a nod and moving on is usually enough.
- If someone asks you to delete a photograph, deleting it is cheap.
- Photographing people who are struggling, without their knowledge, deserves more thought than it
  usually gets.

None of this is a legal requirement. It is just the difference between being a photographer and
being a nuisance.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Set the camera up before you go: f/8, auto ISO, minimum 1/250, continuous focus.
- Find a good background and wait rather than hunting complete scenes.
- Get closer than feels comfortable.
- Know the law where you are, and be decent regardless of it.

</KeyTakeaways>
