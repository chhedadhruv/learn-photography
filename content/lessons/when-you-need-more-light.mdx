"Not enough light" is usually two different problems: not enough total light, or too much contrast
between the lit and shadowed parts. They have different answers.

## Move before you add

The cheapest lighting change is position.

- Turn the subject towards a window rather than away from it.
- Move them nearer to it — light falls off very quickly with distance.
- Find a pale wall to stand them beside; it will bounce light back into the shadow side for free.

A window is a large, soft source. A person a metre from one is beautifully lit; the same person four
metres away is not.

## Fill the shadows

When contrast is the problem rather than quantity, you need <GlossaryLink term="fill-light" />.

Anything pale and reflective works: a sheet of white card, a book, a light jacket, a wall. Held below
and to the shadow side of a face, it lifts the darkness without becoming a second light.

The aim is to reduce contrast, not remove it. A face lit evenly from both sides looks flat.

<Callout type="note">
  A commercial reflector is a convenient piece of white and silver fabric. A piece of foam board
  from a stationer's does the same job for a fraction of the price.
</Callout>

## Then flash

<GlossaryLink term="flash" /> fires for a fraction of a millisecond, bright enough to matter even in
daylight.

Two things to know:

**Bounce it.** Pointed directly at a subject it is a small, hard source and looks it. Angled at a
ceiling or a wall, the whole surface becomes the source, and the light turns soft. This single change
is most of the difference between snapshot lighting and something considered.

**Its best use is outdoors.** Not lighting dark rooms, but filling shadows in bright sun — the
situation where faces go half dark and no amount of exposure adjustment fixes both ends.

## What not to bother with first

A second flash, light stands, umbrellas and modifiers all make sense once you know what you are
trying to do. Bought before that, they mostly sit in a cupboard.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Repositioning the subject relative to a window solves most indoor lighting problems.
- Light falls off very quickly with distance — closer to the window is much brighter.
- A piece of white card fills shadows as well as a bought reflector.
- Bounce flash rather than pointing it; its best use is filling shadows in daylight.

</KeyTakeaways>
