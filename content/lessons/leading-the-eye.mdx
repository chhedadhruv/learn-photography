A photograph is looked at in an order, whether or not you chose one. Composition is largely about
choosing it.

## Lines

The eye follows lines. A road, a fence, a shadow's edge, a row of windows — all of them pull
attention along their length.

A <GlossaryLink term="leading-lines" /> arriving at the subject makes a photograph feel deliberate.
A strong line leaving the frame takes the viewer's attention out with it.

Diagonals carry more energy than horizontals, which is part of why photographing a scene from
slightly off-square so often improves it.

## Frames within the frame

Something in the foreground enclosing the subject — an archway, a window, overhanging branches, a
gap between two people — does two things. It isolates the subject, and it adds a sense of depth by
establishing something nearer than them.

It need not be a literal frame. Anything that surrounds works.

## Layers

A photograph is flat. Depth has to be implied, and the most reliable way is to give the eye
something at more than one distance: foreground, subject, background.

This is why standing back and including something near the camera so often rescues a landscape that
looked better in life than in the photograph.

<Callout type="tip">
  Depth and <GlossaryLink term="depth-of-field" /> are unrelated. Layers imply depth by
  arrangement; depth of field controls what is sharp. A photograph can have both, either or
  neither.
</Callout>

## Where it goes wrong

- A strong line pointing at nothing.
- A frame so dark it becomes a hole rather than a border.
- Foreground included for its own sake, cluttering rather than layering.

The test is always the same: does this give the eye somewhere to go, or something to trip over?

<KeyTakeaways>

- The eye follows lines; make sure they arrive at the subject rather than leaving the frame.
- Foreground elements that enclose the subject add isolation and depth at once.
- Depth is implied by arranging things at different distances.
- Every device should give the eye a route, not an obstacle.

</KeyTakeaways>
